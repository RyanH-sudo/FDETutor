<Section label="The Opening">

Volume 11 is career strategy. The first lesson is the company landscape — who's hiring FDEs in 2026, what each one specifically wants, and how to think about targeting.

</Section>

<Section label="Frontier AI labs">

**Anthropic.** Hiring "Forward Deployed Engineer" and "Solutions Architect" titles. Strong mission alignment around AI safety. Comp at the top of the range. Customer base: largest enterprises in regulated industries. Engagements deep, technical, strategic.

**OpenAI.** Hiring "Forward Deployed Engineer" with regional variants. Founded the FDE program at frontier labs in early 2024. Customer base similar to Anthropic but broader. Engagements often agentic-focused.

**Google DeepMind.** Hiring increasingly through Google Cloud's AI sales engineering and through DeepMind's enterprise team. Less "FDE" branding; more "Customer Engineering" titles. Strong infrastructure depth.

**xAI.** Smaller program. More flexible. Customer base concentrated in specific verticals (defense, government, automotive).

</Section>

<Section label="AI-native startups (Tier 1)">

Companies with significant funding, established product-market fit, hiring FDE-shape roles aggressively:

- **Glean** (enterprise search/work AI). Hiring "Founding Forward Deployed Engineer" titles. Strong customer engagement focus.
- **Vanta** (compliance automation). Hiring "Senior Software Engineer, AI Product" — FDE-shaped. Compliance-heavy.
- **Cresta** (AI for contact centers). Hiring "Senior FDE AI Agent". Conversational AI focus.
- **Decagon** (AI customer support). Hiring "Applied AI Engineer / FDE". High-frequency customer engagement.
- **Reflection AI** (agentic systems). Founding FDE roles open. Heavy on agentic patterns.
- **Harvey** (legal AI). Hiring FDE roles for top law firm engagements. Domain-specific.
- **Hebbia** (financial-services AI). Customer engagements with banks and asset managers.
- **Sierra** (AI customer experience). Hiring across FDE and applied AI tracks.
- **Drata** (compliance AI). Senior AI engineering roles, compliance-adjacent.
- **Sourcegraph** (code AI). Solutions engineering for enterprise code search and AI tools.

Each of these has a distinct vertical or product wedge. Targeting one means understanding their specific customer base.

</Section>

<Section label="AI-adjacent startups (Tier 2)">

Companies building AI features into existing products:

- **Ramp** (fintech with AI). Solutions engineering plus FDE work for largest customers.
- **Linear** (project management with AI). Solutions roles for enterprise customers.
- **Notion** (productivity with AI). Customer engineering for AI features.
- **Figma** (design with AI). Design-AI integration roles.

These roles are often less "FDE" and more "solutions architect with AI specialty." The compensation is generally lower than tier-1 startups but the work can be excellent.

</Section>

<Section label="Traditional enterprises building AI programs">

Companies with mature products adding AI features and hiring for the customer-facing technical work:

- **Databricks** (data + AI platform). Strong FDE program for largest customers.
- **Snowflake** (data cloud). Solutions architecture and FDE roles for enterprise.
- **Microsoft, Google, AWS Cloud.** Customer engineering roles for AI services. More structured, less startup-flavored.
- **IBM, SAP, Oracle, Salesforce.** Solutions engineering roles for their AI offerings. Mature, enterprise-focused.

These pay solid compensation but the equity upside is less. The work can be steady and the customer relationships can be long-term.

</Section>

<Section label="Targeting your search">

Don't apply to everything. Target deliberately. A few questions to ask yourself:

- *What customer base do I want to work with?* Healthcare? Finance? Government? Retail? Pick the vertical and target companies serving that vertical.
- *How much chaos can I handle?* Frontier labs and early startups are intense; established enterprises are more structured.
- *How important is compensation vs equity vs mission?* Tradeoffs are real. Top base salaries are at frontier labs; biggest equity upside is at early startups; most stability is at established enterprises.
- *What technical depth do I bring?* Frontier labs need depth. Some startups will accept breadth for lower comp.

A reasonable strategy: pick 8-12 companies across two or three of these tiers. Apply to all. Take whatever interviews happen. Decide based on what you learn.

</Section>

<Section label="The application timeline">

A realistic timeline from "I'm starting to apply" to "I'm starting a new job":

- Week 1-2: First applications. Prepare materials. Submit 5-10 to start.
- Week 3-4: First recruiter responses. Most positive responses come in this window.
- Week 5-8: Phone screens. Hiring manager interviews. First on-sites.
- Week 8-12: On-sites continue. Multiple companies in flight.
- Week 12-14: Offers start arriving.
- Week 14-16: Negotiation. Final decision.
- Week 16-20: Notice at current job. Start new role.

Plan for 4-5 months from start to start. Sometimes faster (especially if a company really wants you and moves fast). Sometimes slower (especially if you're picky about which company).

</Section>

<FDEConnection>

The single most important targeting decision: don't apply broadly. Apply specifically. Customize each application to the company's actual product, customer base, and stated needs. A generic FDE application gets generic responses; a customized one gets serious interviews.

The cost of customization is real (an extra hour per application). The yield is much higher. Senior FDEs who've done this consistently report ~30-40% positive response rates on customized applications, vs ~5-10% on generic ones.

</FDEConnection>
