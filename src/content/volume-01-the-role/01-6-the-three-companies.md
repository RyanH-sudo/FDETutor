<Section label="The Opening">

Companies that hire FDEs come in three rough archetypes. Knowing which archetype a company is helps you target your application, customize your cover letter, and answer interview questions specifically.

</Section>

<Section label="Archetype 1: Frontier AI labs">

Examples: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, xAI, Meta FAIR-derived teams.

These companies are building the most capable foundation models. They have a small number of huge enterprise customers (Fortune 500, governments, major banks, healthcare systems). FDE engagements are intense, technically deep, and high-stakes — the customer is paying $1M-$50M annually and expects the lab's best people on their account.

What they want from FDEs:
- *Deep technical depth.* You'll be the customer's window into the lab's most advanced capabilities.
- *Production-grade engineering.* Customer deployments must work reliably.
- *Trust-building skill.* Customers at this level are skeptical and powerful. You have to earn confidence.
- *Strategic mindset.* You're often advising on AI strategy, not just deploying.
- *Mission alignment.* Each lab has a specific mission (Anthropic on safety, OpenAI on AGI development, DeepMind on scientific frontier). Cultural fit matters.

Hiring is selective. Comp is highest. Frontier labs are the prestige path.

</Section>

<Section label="Archetype 2: AI-native startups">

Examples: Glean, Vanta, Cresta, Decagon, Reflection AI, Harvey, Hebbia, Sierra, Drata, Sourcegraph.

These companies are building AI products on top of foundation models. Their customer base is broader (mid-market through enterprise). FDE engagements are typically shorter than at frontier labs but still significant.

What they want from FDEs:
- *Hands-on shipping.* You'll write a lot of code. Less strategic, more execution.
- *Customer-relationship skill.* Customers at mid-market level often have less internal AI expertise. You'll teach as well as deploy.
- *Domain depth.* Many of these companies focus on a vertical (legal, customer-support, security, finance). Domain knowledge in that vertical is a strong advantage.
- *Speed.* Startups iterate fast. Engagements that take a quarter at a frontier lab take a month here.
- *Founder-mentality.* You're often shaping the company's processes as much as serving customers. Initiative is valued.

Hiring is selective but the bar is set per company — some startups will hire FDEs with strong builder backgrounds even without prior AI specialization. Comp is strong; equity is a bigger fraction.

</Section>

<Section label="Archetype 3: Traditional enterprises adding FDE programs">

Examples: Databricks, Snowflake, Stripe, Ramp, established SaaS companies launching AI features.

These companies have established products and large customer bases. They've added FDE programs (often within Solutions Architecture or Customer Engineering organizations) because their customers need help integrating new AI features into existing workflows.

What they want from FDEs:
- *Integration expertise.* You'll be helping customers integrate the company's product with their existing systems — APIs, data pipelines, identity providers.
- *Standard-deployment fluency.* Less custom-everything than a frontier lab; more "deploy this pattern correctly."
- *Customer-management at scale.* Often you'll handle 5-10 customers concurrently, vs the 2-3 at frontier labs.
- *Cross-functional collaboration.* Tight coordination with sales, customer success, support.

Hiring is broader. Bar is more conventional (years of experience, vendor certifications). Comp is solid but lower than frontier labs at the senior level.

</Section>

<Section label="How to choose">

Each archetype has different rhythms, different demands, different rewards.

- *If you want to work on the frontier of AI capability:* frontier lab.
- *If you want maximum equity upside and founder-adjacent experience:* AI-native startup.
- *If you want stability, broad customer exposure, and a mature support structure:* traditional enterprise.

Many FDEs spend time at multiple archetypes over their careers. The skills transfer well.

</Section>

<Section label="Targeting your application">

When you apply, customize per archetype:

**Frontier lab applications.** Lead with mission alignment + production AI experience. Show you understand the lab's specific positioning. Cite their public research or engineering blog posts.

**Startup applications.** Lead with shipping speed + customer-facing experience. Show you've operated in scrappy environments. Cite the company's specific product wedge.

**Enterprise applications.** Lead with integration depth + breadth across systems. Show you've handled large customer environments. Cite specific certifications or major-vendor experience.

Same résumé, different cover-letter framings. The cover letter is more important than people think — it's where you show you understand which archetype you're applying to.

</Section>
