<Section label="The Opening">

FDE roles are well-paid. They're paid well because the talent supply is small relative to demand, and because the role's value to the company is direct — successful FDE engagements close large contracts and surface high-leverage product feedback.

This lesson lays out the compensation landscape so you can interpret offers and negotiate clearly. Numbers are 2026 US ranges; international varies considerably.

</Section>

<Section label="The four components of total comp">

A typical AI-lab FDE offer has four elements:

1. **Base salary.** Cash, paid in regular paychecks. The most stable component.
2. **Equity.** Stock options or RSUs. At public companies, market-priced. At private companies, paper value with uncertain liquidity.
3. **Signing bonus.** One-time cash, paid in the first year (sometimes split across two years).
4. **Annual bonus.** Performance-tied, often 10-25% of base.

Total compensation = base + (equity / vesting period) + (signing bonus / years vesting) + annual bonus.

When recruiters quote "$350K total comp," they usually mean year-1 total: base + signing bonus + first-year equity vesting + target annual bonus.

</Section>

<Section label="Frontier AI labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind)">

Approximate 2026 ranges for FDE / Applied AI Engineer roles at frontier labs:

| Level | Base | Total comp (year 1) |
|-------|------|---------------------|
| Mid-level FDE (3-5 yrs experience) | $200-260K | $300-450K |
| Senior FDE (5-8 yrs) | $250-320K | $400-600K |
| Staff / Principal FDE (8+ yrs) | $300-400K | $550-800K+ |

Equity at these companies is significant. Anthropic's last public valuation was $61.5B (March 2025); OpenAI's was $300B (early 2025). Equity grants are paper-valued at these companies, but the secondary market liquidity has improved.

Signing bonuses commonly $50-150K at the senior+ level. Annual bonus targets 10-15%.

</Section>

<Section label="Tier-1 AI startups (Glean, Vanta, Cresta, Decagon, Reflection AI, Harvey, Hebbia, Sierra)">

Approximate 2026 ranges:

| Level | Base | Total comp (year 1) |
|-------|------|---------------------|
| Mid-level FDE | $170-220K | $250-380K |
| Senior FDE | $220-290K | $350-550K |
| Staff/Principal | $280-380K | $500-750K |

Equity at these companies is in the form of stock options (with strike prices) or RSUs. The expected value depends heavily on whether the company successfully exits — IPO or acquisition. Some of these companies (Glean, Sierra) have raised at >$5B valuations; others are smaller.

Signing bonuses smaller, often $25-75K. Annual bonus less common; some startups only pay base + equity.

</Section>

<Section label="Mid-stage and growth-stage startups">

A wide range. Mid-stage AI startups (~Series B/C) typically:

| Level | Base | Total comp (year 1) |
|-------|------|---------------------|
| Mid-level | $150-200K | $200-320K |
| Senior | $190-260K | $300-450K |

Equity is a larger fraction at earlier-stage companies — but more uncertain. Sometimes a startup's equity is worth nothing; sometimes it's worth multiples of an established company's. The variance is real.

</Section>

<Section label="Reading an offer">

A few things to look for in any offer:

**Base salary.** This is your floor. If everything else fails, this is what you get. Don't accept a base that doesn't cover your expenses comfortably even if the equity is huge.

**Equity grant size and vesting.** Standard is 4-year vest with 1-year cliff (no equity until year 1, then 25% at the cliff, then monthly). Some companies (OpenAI, recently) have moved to no-cliff vesting, which is significantly more generous. Read the vesting schedule carefully.

**Equity strike price (for options).** Options have a strike price you pay to convert them to stock. Lower strike = more upside. Compare to the company's current 409A valuation.

**Refresh grants.** Many companies refresh equity annually, so your effective comp stays competitive after year 1. Some don't. Ask about the refresh schedule before accepting.

**Signing bonus clawback.** Most signing bonuses have a clawback provision — if you leave within 1-2 years, you have to pay it back. Read the terms.

**Annual bonus structure.** Is it discretionary or formulaic? Has the company hit its target bonus payout in recent years? Ask.

</Section>

<Section label="Negotiation, briefly">

Always negotiate. Recruiters expect it. The first offer is rarely the best the company will do.

The main levers, in order of typical flexibility:
1. **Signing bonus.** Often the most flexible. One-time cost; doesn't affect the company's salary bands.
2. **Equity.** Often flexible at senior+ levels. Less flexible at junior.
3. **Base salary.** Less flexible — companies have salary bands per level. But not impossible to move.
4. **Level.** If you can argue for a higher level (Senior vs Mid), the band-shift gives you more of everything.

The negotiation language: "I'm excited about the role. The compensation is close to what I'd need to accept. Specifically, I'd like to see the [signing bonus / equity / base] at [number]. Is that possible?"

Don't be vague. Ask for specific numbers. The recruiter has more flexibility than they initially indicate.

</Section>

<Callout kind="tip" title="Use Levels.fyi">

Levels.fyi is the public database of tech compensation. It has self-reported FDE / Applied AI Engineer offers from most major companies. Search for the specific company and level before going into a negotiation. Knowing what others have received calibrates expectations.

</Callout>

<FDEConnection>

When negotiating, always negotiate from value, not need. "I would need $X to accept" is a weak frame. "Based on market data and the impact I expect to have, I'd like to see $X" is a strong frame.

The work you'll do as an FDE — large customer engagements, significant product influence, customer trust building — is high-leverage. Comp should reflect the leverage, not your personal financial situation.

</FDEConnection>
