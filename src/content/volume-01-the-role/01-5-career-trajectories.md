<Section label="The Opening">

A common interview question: "where do you see yourself in five years?" The honest answer for an FDE depends on which trajectory appeals. There are several. Each is real and well-trod.

</Section>

<Section label="Track 1: Principal-track FDE / Distinguished Engineer">

The senior individual-contributor track. You stay an FDE but grow in scope and influence. After 5-10 years, you're a Principal FDE or Distinguished Engineer. You handle the most complex customer engagements (often the largest by ACV), set technical direction across the FDE team, write the internal patterns other FDEs follow, and influence the platform roadmap heavily.

Comp at this level (frontier AI labs): $400-700K base, $700K-$1.5M total comp. Mostly via equity refreshes that keep growing as the company grows.

This is the path for engineers who love the work and don't want to manage people. It pays well. It's high-leverage. It's a small population at every company — typically 5-15 distinguished/principal-level FDEs at a 200-FDE org.

</Section>

<Section label="Track 2: FDE Leadership / Manager">

The management track. After 3-5 years as an IC FDE, you become a team lead, then an engineering manager, then a director, then potentially a VP.

The work changes shape. Less hands-on engagement. More coaching FDEs. More cross-functional coordination with sales, product, customer success. More executive-level visibility.

Comp similar at the senior levels but with more variance — some managers earn less than peer ICs at the same level; some earn more. Depends on the company and the manager's reach.

This path requires you to enjoy mentoring, scheduling, performance management, and the ambiguity of being responsible for outcomes you produce through others. Some FDEs love this. Some hate it.

</Section>

<Section label="Track 3: Founder">

The most common quiet outcome for FDEs. After 3-7 years of customer-embedded work, you've seen dozens of customers, dozens of patterns, dozens of unmet needs. You've built relationships with people who would buy from you. You have a strong sense of what's missing in the market.

A surprising fraction of FDEs eventually leave to start their own companies. Often these are technical-product startups with the FDE serving as the technical co-founder; sometimes solo or with a non-technical partner.

Notable founders with FDE backgrounds:
- Several Palantir FDEs went on to found AI/data startups in the 2015-2025 window.
- The first OpenAI FDE team has already produced spinouts.

If you have founder ambitions, FDE work is excellent training. You see what real customers actually need. You learn to scope and ship. You build a network of people who will trust you when you call asking for advice or money.

</Section>

<Section label="Track 4: Platform engineering pivot">

Some FDEs pivot back into product/platform engineering after a few years. The customer-facing work is intense; sometimes engineers want a calmer rhythm.

The pivot is well-respected because FDEs come back to platform engineering with a deep understanding of how the platform is actually used. They become some of the best product engineers because they've seen the whole pipeline. The friction with this pivot: comp can drop slightly (FDE pays a premium for customer skill), and the rhythm is genuinely different.

</Section>

<Section label="Track 5: Customer-success leadership / Field leadership">

A track where you become the head of customer success, head of solutions architecture, or VP of field engineering at the same company or a different one. Less hands-on technical, more organizational. Often paired with significant equity or revenue-share comp at smaller companies.

This is a less common path but real. Some former FDEs find they're better suited to large-scale customer-facing leadership than to either hands-on engineering or pure people-management of engineers.

</Section>

<Section label="Track 6: Industry advisor / fractional CTO">

Some senior FDEs eventually move to advisory work. They sit on boards, consult with several companies in parallel, and trade hands-on engagement for breadth. This is more common after 10+ years and often combines with founder tracks.

</Section>

<Section label="What the tracks share">

What's common across all of them: deep customer experience, technical credibility, network. These compound throughout the FDE career. After three years of FDE work, you have the customer pattern recognition, the technical references, and the personal network that make any of these tracks accessible.

The choice between tracks usually doesn't have to be made early. Most FDEs spend their first 2-3 years as senior ICs, then choose direction based on what they've discovered they enjoy. Don't pre-commit. Let the work tell you.

</Section>
